# Research framing

forenix-oss is a research-grade open-source platform that
operationalises a specific empirical question: **how can OSINT
pipelines be structured to produce trustworthy, legally admissible
evidence while safeguarding fundamental rights?**

The codebase is also a working product (see [`README.md`](README.md)
for the product surface and [`docs/SAAS.md`](docs/SAAS.md) for the
commercial deployment that funds further development). The
commercial story is real; this document is for readers who want to
engage with the platform as a research artefact rather than as a
tool to deploy.

If that is you, this page is the right entry door.

---

## 1. The research problem

OSINT (open-source intelligence) collection and forensic case
management are siloed in practice. Analysts run collection tools
(Maigret, Sherlock, Subfinder, theHarvester) in one workstation,
copy findings into a separate case-management system (CaseGuard,
X-Ways, locally maintained spreadsheets), and reconstruct
chain-of-custody after the fact. The handoff is manual; the chain
is fragile; and the resulting evidence rarely survives the
admissibility tests that courts in jurisdictions with strong
evidence rules (Canada, the EU, the UK) actually apply.

Two further problems sit underneath:

1. **The epistemic problem.** OSINT pipelines that rely on
   large language models risk producing plausible-but-unsupported
   findings. The intelligence-studies literature (Heuer 1999;
   Heuer and Pherson 2010+; Coulthart 2017) is clear that human
   analysts under cognitive load default to confirmation bias and
   anchoring; software inheriting their workflow inherits the same
   pathologies unless it imposes a structured analytic discipline.

2. **The rights problem.** Open data is not licence-free data.
   Scraping public profiles, harvesting breach corpora, and
   correlating identity fragments across platforms raises distinct
   legal questions under PIPEDA (Canada), GDPR (EU), and the
   Fourth-Amendment doctrine after *Carpenter v. United States*
   (2018) in the US. A pipeline that ignores these constraints by
   default is one whose outputs cannot be relied on in formal
   proceedings.

forenix-oss is the platform built to test interventions against
these three problems together.

## 2. Methodology: design-science research

The work follows the design-science research paradigm (Hevner et
al. 2004; Peffers et al. 2007). The **artefact** is the platform.
The **research output** is the design knowledge generated by
building, deploying, and stressing it against real cases.

A design-science PhD or research programme using forenix-oss would
typically deliver three categories of contribution:

| Category | Form | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **The artefact** | the codebase itself  -  reusable, MIT-licensed | this repository |
| **Design principles** | extracted from the design decisions encoded in the artefact (the audit-chain choice, the SAT trace requirement, the per-case Git repo) | academic papers + ADR set in [`docs/06-ARCHITECTURE.md`](docs/06-ARCHITECTURE.md) |
| **Empirical evaluation** | case studies running the platform against real targets, measured against legal-admissibility and SAT-fidelity criteria | future publications; early case studies on Sigstore and Internet Archive landed during 0.5.0 development |

The design-science choice matters because purely conceptual
governance frameworks for OSINT are common (Bellingcat
methodology, Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source
Investigations 2022, EU Joint Investigation Teams handbook) but
**implemented** frameworks that ship as software, with measurable
behaviour against the standards they claim to enforce, are not.
The reference implementation is the contribution.

## 3. Research questions the platform enables

Four open questions sit at the intersection of OSINT engineering
and information law. forenix-oss was built so each can be
investigated empirically rather than only theoretically. Full
literature framing for each is in
[`docs/research/research-questions.md`](docs/research/research-questions.md).

**RQ1.** What governance framework should constrain OSINT
pipelines that aim for legal admissibility, and how does that
framework vary across jurisdictions with materially different
evidence rules (Canada, EU, US)?

**RQ2.** Can structured analytic techniques (Heuer-Pherson,
Coulthart) be operationalised in code without losing their
epistemic function? An SAT performed by software is not
self-evidently the same SAT as one performed by a human; the
question is whether the cognitive corrective survives the
translation.

**RQ3.** What rights-protective design constraints should be built
into OSINT tooling by default, and what is the legal status of
each constraint under PIPEDA, GDPR, and the post-*Carpenter*
Fourth-Amendment doctrine?

**RQ4.** How do cryptographic chain-of-custody primitives (forward
hash chains, transparency logs, cosigned attestations) interact
with existing evidence rules across jurisdictions? Do they
strengthen admissibility, are they treated as ordinary metadata,
or does their cryptographic character create new categories of
expert-witness contest?

## 4. What the platform already lets a researcher do

The platform is past prototype. As of 0.5.0 it ships:

- **A SHA-256 forward-chained audit log** with an offline
  verification recipe (12 lines of Python in [`docs/07-SECURITY.md`](docs/07-SECURITY.md)).
  Every state-changing operation appends a row; the chain is
  globally keyed so the entire history verifies in one pass.

- **Multi-backend attestation** (local HMAC, GitHub-issue witness,
  Sigstore Rekor entry) so audit-log roots can be published to
  external, independently-operated logs. The artefact for an
  external auditor to compare against is the same byte sequence
  the platform persisted internally.

- **A structured-analytic-technique scaffold** (see
  [`docs/10-ANALYTIC_FRAMEWORK.md`](docs/10-ANALYTIC_FRAMEWORK.md))
  that requires every LLM-produced finding to carry a SAT trace.
  Conclusions that arrive without a SAT trace are persisted with
  confidence downgraded to `unverified` and surfaced to the
  operator as such, so epistemic weakness is recorded in the
  evidence chain rather than silently smoothed away.

- **A real Git repository per case**, not a shadow database table.
  Evidence files are committed; branches model competing
  hypotheses; merges model adjudication. The case repository is a
  first-class research dataset and exports cleanly to standard Git
  tooling.

- **A free-adapter substrate** (mock, Ollama, GLM, OpenRouter,
  NVIDIA, Groq) so the same pipeline can be replayed against
  different LLMs to control for model-specific artefacts in
  empirical work.

The artefact does not, by design, pre-decide answers to RQ1-RQ4.
It is the apparatus, not the conclusion.

## 5. Where the platform has been stressed

Two case studies have been run against the live deployment at
demo.forenix.tech and are preserved in the repository's case
archive:

| Target | Investigation type | Findings | Entities | Report artefact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `archive.org` | Public-infrastructure footprint + incident retrospective | 11 | 12 | Bridged to forensic case, PDF exported |
| `sigstore.dev` | Transparency-log + signing-infrastructure footprint | 5 | 11 | Bridged to forensic case, PDF exported |

Both ran the full pipeline (analyse, extract entities, generate
report, bridge to case, render admissible PDF). Both were
end-to-end audited and ended with verifiable chain-of-custody.

These are early-stage demonstrations, not formal evaluations. They
establish that the apparatus works against live targets; they do
not yet measure performance against admissibility criteria in any
jurisdiction. That measurement is the next research output.

## 6. Disciplinary positioning

forenix-oss sits between four established disciplines:

- **Intelligence studies** (Coulthart, Heuer, Pherson, Marrin):
  the SAT scholarship the platform implements.
- **Information law and data governance** (broad literature on
  PIPEDA, GDPR, the Berkeley Protocol, post-*Carpenter* doctrine):
  the legal frame the platform is built to satisfy.
- **Digital forensics and evidence law** (Mason and Seng 2017;
  Casey 2011): the chain-of-custody literature the audit chain and
  attestation backends translate into software.
- **Software engineering research** (Hevner et al. 2004; Wieringa
  2014): the design-science methodology under which the artefact
  itself counts as scholarly output.

A doctoral programme using forenix-oss would draw a supervisory
committee from at least two of these disciplines. The technical
implementation is mature enough that the doctoral contribution
sits primarily in (1) and (2): governance framework first,
artefact second.

## 7. Citing forenix-oss

If you use forenix-oss in academic work, please cite the
repository at its current release tag and note the commit SHA
your analysis ran against. A machine-readable
[`CITATION.cff`](CITATION.cff) is provided at the repository
root for Zotero, Mendeley, and other reference managers that
consume the Citation File Format.

Tagged releases are archived on Zenodo via the GitHub integration.
For formal citation prefer the DOI over the GitHub URL.

- **Concept DOI** (always resolves to the most recent version):
  [10.5281/zenodo.20329059](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20329059)
- **Version DOI for v0.5.2**:
  [10.5281/zenodo.20329060](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20329060)

A working citation form for prose use:

```
Bhutto, A. M. (2026). forenix-oss: an open-source platform for
lawful OSINT and forensic case management (Version 0.5.2)
[Computer software]. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20329060
```

## 9. Methodology cross-references

For peer review, the design claims in this document are checkable
against the implementation at the following locations:

- **SHA-256 forward chain.** Hash primitive at
  [`src/lib/audit-chain.ts`](src/lib/audit-chain.ts); the
  Prisma-backed wrapper that performs the append is at
  [`src/lib/audit.ts`](src/lib/audit.ts). The chain is verified
  end-to-end by [`scripts/verify-audit-chain.ts`](scripts/verify-audit-chain.ts)
  and reimplemented in twelve lines of Python in
  [`docs/07-SECURITY.md`](docs/07-SECURITY.md) section 4.

- **External attestation backends.** Common interface at
  [`src/lib/attestation/types.ts`](src/lib/attestation/types.ts);
  three implementations under
  [`src/lib/attestation/backends/`](src/lib/attestation/backends/)
  (local HMAC, GitHub-issue witness, Sigstore Rekor). Each has a
  paired `*.test.ts` covering round-trip.

- **SAT discipline.** Schema for the trace at
  [`src/lib/ai/types.ts`](src/lib/ai/types.ts); the prompts that
  enforce structured output at
  [`src/lib/ai/sat-prompts.ts`](src/lib/ai/sat-prompts.ts);
  schema conformance and the confidence-downgrade behaviour for
  non-conforming output are handled in the pipeline runner at
  [`src/app/api/pipeline/run/[id]/route.ts`](src/app/api/pipeline/run/%5Bid%5D/route.ts).

- **Real Git per case.** Engine at
  [`src/lib/git-engine.ts`](src/lib/git-engine.ts); evidence
  storage at [`src/lib/evidence-store.ts`](src/lib/evidence-store.ts).
  Tests at `src/lib/git-engine.test.ts` and `evidence-store.test.ts`.

- **Free-adapter substrate.** Common interface at
  [`src/lib/ai/types.ts`](src/lib/ai/types.ts); factory at
  [`src/lib/ai/adapter.ts`](src/lib/ai/adapter.ts); the six
  implementations under [`src/lib/ai/adapters/`](src/lib/ai/adapters/)
  (mock, ollama, glm, openrouter, nvidia, groq).

- **Multi-tenant scope helper.** Decision matrix at
  [`src/lib/rbac.ts`](src/lib/rbac.ts) (function `teamScopeWhere`);
  JWT plumbing at [`src/auth.ts`](src/auth.ts).

## 10. Pre-registration

Each of the empirical questions in
[`docs/research/research-questions.md`](docs/research/research-questions.md)
is the kind of study that benefits from pre-registration before
data collection (the Open Science Framework, AsPredicted, or a
discipline-specific equivalent). The project's intent for any
substantive empirical work running on the platform is to
pre-register the protocol and the analysis plan before the first
case-study run. Replication artefacts (configuration, prompts,
adapter version, seed) are expected to be committed alongside the
study outputs.
